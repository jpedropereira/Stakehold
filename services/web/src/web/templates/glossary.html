Glossary

What each figure on your holdings, in Labs and on Risk means. Amounts are in euros unless a figure says otherwise.

Units
How many shares of the holding you own. An account's own page calls this Quantity.
Avg cost
What you paid per unit, averaged over your buys and including fees. Each buy is converted to euros at the exchange rate on the day you made it, so the euro average and the trading-currency average aren't linked by any single rate.
Cost basis
What you paid for the units you still hold: units × avg cost. Exchange rates moving after a buy don't change it.
Market value
What the units you hold are worth at the latest closing price. The smaller line beneath is the same value in the currency the holding trades in. It says “Mixed currencies” when your accounts hold the ticker in different currencies.
Weight
The holding's share of the market value of all your priced holdings. A holding still waiting for prices has no weight, and isn't counted as worth zero.
Unrealized return
Market value − cost basis: how much the price has moved since you bought. It leaves out dividends. The percentage is that amount ÷ cost basis.
Dividends received
The dividends paid to you, net of withholding tax where your broker records the tax against the dividend.
Total return
Unrealized return + dividends received: what the holding has made for you so far. The percentage is that amount ÷ cost basis, so it measures what your money made, and depends on when and how much you bought.
Prices as of
The trading day of the latest closing price used. Prices update daily, and returns are recalculated a few times a day.
Backtest
How a mix of assets would have done if you had bought it at the start of each period and held it until the latest day every asset has a price. Labs starts from your current holdings, with today's weights. Returns are in euros, include dividends, and are percentages only: how much you hold plays no part.
Buy and hold
The mix is bought at its weights on the period's first day and never rebalanced, so an asset that rises comes to weigh more. Each period starts again from the same weights on its own first day.
Max
Each asset's whole history. For the mix, it starts on the first day the oldest asset has a price, and each other asset joins on its own first day, when the mix is split again by the weights you set. A shorter period an asset doesn't cover shows “Not enough history” for that asset, and the mix leaves it out of that period.

Ex-post
Measured on what you actually held: the daily time-weighted return of your invested holdings, in euros with dividends included. Buying and selling change the next day's weights, not the return. Cash isn't tracked yet, so it plays no part. These are historical figures for each window, which ends on the latest day every holding has a price.
Cumulative return
How much the portfolio grew or shrank over the whole window, compounding each day's return.
CAGR
The compound annual return: the steady yearly return that would have turned the window's start into its end. Over a window shorter than three years a few good or bad months can move it a lot.
Volatility
How much the weekly returns swung around their average, scaled to a year (the standard deviation × √52). Higher means a bumpier ride, in either direction.
Downside deviation
Like volatility, but counting only the weeks that did worse than the risk-free rate (the euro overnight rate, €STR). It measures the bumps that hurt.
Max drawdown
The largest fall from a high point to the low that followed, within the window. The dates show the peak, the trough and when the portfolio got back to the peak, if it has.
Sharpe ratio
The return above the risk-free rate for each unit of volatility, from weekly returns over the window. Higher means more return for the swings taken. It's an estimate from a limited history: the ± figure is one standard error (1 s.e.), and with three years of data it is often about ±0.6, so two Sharpe ratios that close can't be told apart.
Sortino ratio
Like the Sharpe ratio, but dividing by the downside deviation, so only the bad weeks count as risk.
Calmar ratio
CAGR ÷ the size of the max drawdown: how much yearly return there was for the worst fall endured.
1-day VaR
Value at risk: a one-day loss the portfolio's daily returns exceeded on only 5% of days (95%) or 1% of days (99%) in the window. It is a historical threshold, not a limit: worse days happened.
1-day CVaR
Conditional value at risk: the average loss on the days beyond the VaR, the worst 5% or 1% of days. It says how bad those days were, where VaR only says where they begin.
