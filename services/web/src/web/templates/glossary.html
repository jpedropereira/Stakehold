Glossary

What each figure on your holdings and in Labs means. Amounts are in euros unless a figure says otherwise.

Units
How many shares of the holding you own. An account's own page calls this Quantity.
Avg cost
What you paid per unit, averaged over your buys and including fees. Each buy is converted to euros at the exchange rate on the day you made it, so the euro average and the trading-currency average aren't linked by any single rate.
Cost basis
What you paid for the units you still hold: units × avg cost. Exchange rates moving after a buy don't change it.
Market value
What the units you hold are worth at the latest closing price. The smaller line beneath is the same value in the currency the holding trades in. It says “Mixed currencies” when your accounts hold the ticker in different currencies.
Weight
The holding's share of the market value of all your priced holdings. A holding still waiting for prices has no weight, and isn't counted as worth zero.
Unrealized return
Market value − cost basis: how much the price has moved since you bought. It leaves out dividends. The percentage is that amount ÷ cost basis.
Dividends received
The dividends paid to you, net of withholding tax where your broker records the tax against the dividend.
Total return
Unrealized return + dividends received: what the holding has made for you so far. The percentage is that amount ÷ cost basis, so it measures what your money made, and depends on when and how much you bought.
Prices as of
The trading day of the latest closing price used. Prices update daily, and returns are recalculated a few times a day.
Backtest
How a mix of assets would have done if you had bought it at the start of each period and held it until the latest day every asset has a price. Labs starts from your current holdings, with today's weights. Returns are in euros, include dividends, and are percentages only: how much you hold plays no part.
Buy and hold
The mix is bought at its weights on the period's first day and never rebalanced, so an asset that rises comes to weigh more: the weights drift. Each period starts again from the same weights on its own first day. The risk figures are labelled “buy & hold (drifting)” for this reason: they measure the mix as it drifted, not one kept at its starting weights.
Max
Each asset's whole history. For the mix, it starts on the first day the oldest asset has a price, and each other asset joins on its own first day, when the mix is split again by the weights you set. A shorter period an asset doesn't cover shows “Not enough history” for that asset, and the mix leaves it out of that period.
Risk windows
Labs measures risk over the last 1, 3, 5 and 10 years, and over Max, all ending on the latest day every asset has a price. Every row, the mix's and each asset's, uses the same window, so the figures can be compared. That window can start no earlier than the youngest asset's first price, and the page names that asset. A window longer than that history shows “Not enough history”. Weekly figures use whole weeks, Friday to Friday, inside the window.
Returns used
How many daily and weekly returns a window's figures rest on. Fewer returns make a figure less certain. Below a minimum (52 weeks for volatility, Sharpe and Sortino; a year for CAGR, drawdown and Calmar) a figure isn't shown, and its cell says why.
CAGR
The window's historical return as a yearly rate: the return per year that, compounded, gives the window's whole return. In euros, dividends included. Measured on daily returns. It isn't annualised for windows under a year.
Volatility
How much the historical weekly returns varied, scaled to a year: their standard deviation × √52. A higher figure means a bumpier ride. It counts rises and falls alike.
Max drawdown
The largest historical fall from a high to a later low within the window, as a percentage of the high. Measured on daily values. It depends on where the window starts: one that starts mid-fall understates it.
Drawdown
On each day, how far the mix was below its highest value so far in the period. 0% is a new high. The chart shows the historical path the max drawdown is the deepest point of.
Sharpe ratio
The historical return above the risk-free rate per unit of volatility, from weekly returns, per year. The risk-free rate is the euro overnight rate: €STR, and before October 2019 EONIA, adjusted to match. The ± is one standard error: the true figure lies inside it only about two times in three, and it is a lower bound, since real returns have fatter tails than the estimate assumes. Over three years it is about ± 0.6, so two Sharpe ratios that close can't be told apart.
Sortino ratio
As the Sharpe ratio, but dividing by the downside deviation: only the weeks the historical return fell short of the risk-free rate count as risk.
Calmar ratio
Historical CAGR ÷ the size of the max drawdown, over the same window: how much a year the mix made for each unit of its worst fall. Under three years it is marked “under 3 years”, since one bad stretch dominates a short window.
